02 · Positioning & Category Claim

Software fails operations for a structural reason. We translate operations into an ontology before writing code, and we stay embedded to run it.

MOVE 01

Software fails operations for a structural reason.

Most systems are built from a feature list — a description of what someone remembered to ask for, not how the business runs. So software tracks activity instead of enforcing process, and the owner stays the bottleneck.

MOVE 02

The fix is a model, built before an interface.

We translate operations into an ontology: the objects, stages, accountability, and exceptions the business actually runs on. That model is the product. The screens are how people touch it.

MOVE 03

A model is worthless if the builder leaves.

Operations change. So we don’t deliver and depart — we embed as the client’s tech department, guard the stack, and extend the system as the business moves. That is the hover.

How We Behave When It Costs Us Something
01 · PRINCIPLE

Watch before you build

We talk to the people doing the work until we can write one clear sentence about what success means. If we can’t write that sentence, we don’t start.

02 · PRINCIPLE

Model over feature

When a request conflicts with the ontology, we surface the conflict instead of quietly bolting on a field. Features are cheap; an incoherent model is expensive forever.

03 · PRINCIPLE

Scope transparency, both directions

When we exceed contracted scope, we say so in writing and price it at zero. When we can’t deliver something, we say that too — early.

04 · PRINCIPLE

Proof over adjectives

We name systems that are live. We don’t publish case studies for work that hasn’t shipped, and we don’t describe capability we haven’t deployed.

05 · PRINCIPLE

Own the stack, own the outcome

If it’s in our care and it breaks at 2am, it’s ours. No handoff language, no vendor-blame, no ticket theatre.

06 · PRINCIPLE

Leave the client more capable

Documentation, SOPs, and runbooks are deliverables, not favours. A client who understands their own system is a client who stays by choice.

Direct Answers

Clear answers. No fluff.

Why do you require the Discovery Sprint before building?

You can’t automate what you haven’t mapped. The Discovery Sprint translates your operations into a working ontology, proves the model with a working prototype on your real data, and produces a de-risked build plan with fixed pricing.

Who owns the code and intellectual property?

You own 100% of the code, data, and ontology definitions. We build directly on your cloud accounts or provide complete source repository handover with full documentation.

What if we already have fragile or unmaintained software?

Our Persistent Stack Guardian capability handles active codebase rescue and takeover. We map the existing data structures, stabilize operations, create eval suites, and assume 24/7 guardianship.

How does the embedded retainer work after launch?

We don’t hand over logins and leave. Under a monthly SLA-backed retainer, we act as your outsourced tech department — maintaining uptime, executing build queues, and extending the system as your operations evolve.
