How we work

Five steps. Then we start the next five.

This isn't a sales pitch dressed up as a process. It's the actual order we work in — and unlike a one-off project, it doesn't end when step five is done. It just starts again on whatever you need built next.

01

We watch before we touch anything

We talk to the people actually doing the work, not just the owner or manager who called us in — and we watch how things really get done, until we can write one clear sentence: this works if [this specific number] goes from x to y.

02

We start with the smallest thing that proves it works

No giant six-month plan. We pick the smallest, simplest version that proves the idea on your real information, with your real team using it — and we write down everything else we're holding off on for now, so nothing sneaks in later.

03

We check our own work before calling it done

Anywhere this involves AI, we decide what a "right answer" actually looks like — using real examples — before we consider the work finished. AI that hasn't been checked against real examples isn't ready, no matter how good the demo looked.

04

We stick around for the risky part

The first few weeks after something new goes live are when problems actually show up. Not because the work was wrong — because nobody was watching closely enough to catch it before your team did. We watch closely.

05

We stay, and we bring everything we've learned with us

We write down what was specific to this project versus what should become a standard part of how your business runs — then we stay on as the team that keeps it working, supports it, and builds whatever comes next, already knowing everything from this round.

Qualification

We're built for a relationship, not a quick job.

We turn away work that's actually just a one-off job, because that's not what we're set up to do well. Here's how to tell if we're the right long-term fit.

Good fit

  • You want one technical partner for everything — new projects, connecting your systems together, and quick fixes when something breaks — instead of managing a different company for each.
  • You don't have (or don't want) a full in-house engineering team, but you need one to actually exist somewhere.
  • You're tired of re-explaining your business every time a new contractor starts.
  • Your systems and data are complex enough that continuity — someone who already knows where the bodies are buried — is worth more than a cheaper one-off quote.

Not a fit

  • You need one clearly-defined project finished, with no ongoing relationship after.
  • You already have a capable in-house engineering team and just need extra hands short-term.
  • You're shopping purely on lowest hourly rate.
  • Nobody on your side is available to work directly with our team over time.
